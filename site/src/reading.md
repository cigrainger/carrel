# Reading and keeping passages

**Today** collects recent publications. **Reading** holds work in progress.
**Library** contains your archive, including Unread, Starred and Shared with you
filters. **Highlights** brings your saved passages and notes together.

The sidebar holds these destinations and your sources. Assign an optional folder
in Settings → Sources; selecting its name opens a combined chronological list,
and its disclosure button collapses the source rows. `Cmd+B` shows/hides the
sidebar while preserving the visible passage or list row.

## Keep your place

Opening an article remembers the list, search, loaded pages, selection and
scroll position you came from. Return uses that context. **Load older** extends
the current list; there is no automatic infinite scroll.

Reading completion is your action. Opening, scrolling, reaching the end and
ordinary previous/next navigation do not mark an item read. Use the read control
or **Mark read & next** at the article end when you want to complete it.

## Make reading comfortable

**Aa** in the reader's context bar offers font, size, line width, spacing and
Paper/Sepia/Dark/Black themes. Settings → Reading contains the same controls plus
Editorial/Dense lists. Dense uses smaller dates/titles and omits excerpts.
Changes preserve your visible passage or row; the article/list stays mounted
behind Settings. Preferences belong to this installation.

`Cmd+,` opens Settings. `Cmd+K` opens the command palette; `?` shows the effective
keyboard reference, including local overrides. Controls have action labels and
keyboard hints. On platforms using Control, follow the app's displayed hints.

## Search the local archive

Each list can search titles and cached article text offline. Every word must
match, and words match the beginning of indexed words. Punctuation is literal;
there is no search query language to learn. Filters and source/folder selection
still apply. `/` focuses search and `\` clears it. New text indexes may be
prepared after a migration or new cache arrives; titles remain searchable.

## Keep a passage

Select text to highlight or attach a note. Highlights opens the source passage,
shows complete notes and supports editing, deliberate removal and Markdown
export through a native save dialog.

If two of your devices edit the same note offline, Carrel retains both drafts.
Review the conflict explicitly and choose or combine them. It does not silently
discard an independently edited note. An archived passage remains available
when the publisher's page changes or disappears, provided its cache is present.

## Link back to your archive

Use **Copy Carrel link** in reader Info, a highlight row, or the app menu.
`Cmd+Shift+C` copies the current article or the selected article/highlight in a
list. Markdown annotation exports include these links alongside publisher URLs.

Article links use `carrel://item/…`; highlight links use `carrel://highlight/…`
and return to the saved passage. The installed desktop app opens them whether
it is already running or starting up. If a dialog is open, the link waits until
you close it so your draft stays intact. Only the latest waiting link is kept.

These are private archive references. They work on desktops that already have
the content, including your synced desktops. They do not share an article,
grant access or download missing content. Use the sharing flow to send an
article to a trusted person. A removed highlight or missing article leaves
your current reading view in place and explains what is unavailable.

If clipboard access is unavailable, Carrel selects the complete link in a
dialog for ordinary copying. There is no clipboard monitoring.

**Open original** uses your system browser. Archived reading uses local cached
resources; an image that could not be cached is shown as unavailable rather
than fetched remotely by the reader.
