<article class="specimen-article"><p class="specimen-meta">The commonplace · A reading example</p><h2>Some things are<br>worth keeping.</h2><p class="specimen-summary">A sentence underlined. A thought in the margin.<br>The beginnings of a conversation.</p><div class="specimen-rule"></div><p>Reading leaves traces. A phrase comes back to you on a walk. An argument changes the way you see something. You find a passage and know exactly who you want to send it to.</p><p>A commonplace book gives these encounters somewhere to live. <mark>What you keep becomes a record of what you noticed, and what you noticed becomes a way of thinking.</mark></p><p>You don’t need to keep everything. Read from your local archive, even offline, with typography you can make your own.</p><a class="text-link" href="getting-started.html#add-something-to-read">Make yourself at home <span aria-hidden="true">→</span></a></div>
<div class="reading-act"><span class="eyebrow">Keep</span><h2>A commonplace<br>book, quietly growing.</h2><p>Underline a passage. Leave a note. Return to what you noticed, with the archived article for context. Your highlights and notes can leave with you as Markdown.</p><a class="text-link" href="reading.html#keep-a-passage">Reading &amp; marginalia <span aria-hidden="true">→</span></a></div>

<section class="why-carrel" id="why-carrel" aria-labelledby="why-title">
<div class="why-heading"><p class="eyebrow">A small, deliberate corner of the internet</p><h2 id="why-title">Good reading<br>has always been<br>personal.</h2><svg class="large-ribbon" viewBox="0 0 640 520" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><g fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="46"><path d="M574 64H286a196 196 0 0 0 0 392h288"/><path d="M574 144H286a116 116 0 0 0 0 232h288"/><path d="M574 224H286a36 36 0 0 0 0 72h288"/></g><path d="M500 456h74" fill="none" stroke="#ef936c" stroke-width="46"/></svg></div>
<div class="why-prose"><p>There was something good in Google Reader’s small circles: people who read carefully, shared what was worth sharing, and trusted each other’s taste. A recommendation arrived with a person behind it.</p><p>Carrel grows from that memory, and from an older habit: keeping a commonplace book. What you read, what you noticed, and who you wanted to tell belong to the same reading life.</p><p>The open web still gives us the raw material. Blogs still publish. Feeds still work. We want a tool that makes room for that life again, on our own computers, among people we choose.</p><a class="text-link" href="https://github.com/cigrainger/carrel/blob/main/docs/VISION.md">Read the full vision <span aria-hidden="true">↗</span></a></div>
</section>

<section class="commitments" aria-labelledby="commitments-title">
<div class="section-heading"><p class="eyebrow">The shape of the tool</p><h2 id="commitments-title">Yours to read.<br>Yours to keep.</h2></div>
<dl class="commitment-list"><div><dt>At home on your computer.</dt><dd>Your archive lives locally, under your own keys. Reading doesn’t require a Carrel account. Your own paired desktops can exchange private state.</dd></div><div><dt>Small circles, chosen by you.</dt><dd>Sharing goes to a person you explicitly choose. The article and your vouch travel together; your personal highlights, notes and reading state stay private.</dd></div><div><dt>A place you can put down.</dt><dd>No engagement metrics, algorithmic recommendations or notifications outside the app. You decide when something is read, and when you’re done.</dd></div><div><dt>Open, all the way through.</dt><dd>Open source under AGPL-3.0-or-later. OPML for subscriptions, Markdown for your annotations. No telemetry or central Carrel service we operate.</dd></div></dl>
</section>
